# Review of §02.04 "Four Kinds of Behavior"

Reviewed file: /home/claude/review0204/target/02.04.md (line numbers refer to it). Every command printed in the section was re-run (gcc-14 14.2.0 with `-std=c23`, clang 18.1.3, avr-gcc/avr-g++ 7.3.0 + simavr). Scratch files are in /tmp/r0204/w. Note: on the verification machine, plain `gcc` is GCC 13.3, so every run used `gcc-14`.

## 1. Errors

**E1. Line 27. Cross-reference. Severity: medium.**
**Problem:** "§02.03 described the C standard as a contract with two parties."
**Evidence:** The words "contract", "party" and "parties" do not occur in siblings/02.03.md. The contract image comes from §02.02, line 487 ("The philosophy as a contract"), and it has *four* parties: the language, the library, the implementation and the programmer. §02.02 also lists that phrase as its own inference (line 681). The sentence therefore gets both the section and the number wrong.
**Fix:** "§02.02 described C's design as a contract among four parties: the language, the library, the implementation and the programmer. Here two of them matter: the **programmer**, who writes a program, and the **implementation** (a compiler, its libraries and the machine they target), which runs it."

**E2. Line 14. Fact/count. Severity: medium (it is the section's opening hook).**
**Problem:** "gave **four different answers** when the same source file was built four ways: 1 … 0 … 1 … and 4,294,967,296"
**Evidence:** The sentence itself lists 1 twice, so there are three distinct answers. Re-running confirms 1 (GCC 14, at every level from -O0 to -Os), 0 (avr-gcc, all levels), 1 (Clang -O0) and 4294967296 (Clang -O1/-O2/-O3/-Os). Line 301 ("the three shift answers") and Figure 02.04-2 ("three ways") contradict line 14.
**Fix:** "gave **three different answers** when the same source file was built four ways: 1 on your laptop with GCC, 0 on the ATmega328P, 1 again with the Clang compiler without optimization, and 4,294,967,296 with Clang at `-O2` …"

**E3. Line 306 (also Self-check 10 at line 530 and the Orbit box at line 416). Instruction-level fact. Severity: medium.**
**Problem:** "with a divisor of 0 that procedure simply produces a quotient of −1 (all bits 1) and a remainder equal to the dividend."
**Evidence:** /tmp/r0204/w/dz2.c was built with avr-gcc -Os and run on simavr. It printed `7/0=-1 7%0=7`, `-7/0=1 -7%0=-7`, `0/0=-1 0%0=0`, `32767/0=-1` and `-32768/0=1 -32768%0=-32768`. `__divmodhi4` divides the magnitudes (the unsigned routine gives 0xFFFF) and then negates the quotient when the operands' signs differ. A divisor of 0 counts as non-negative, so a negative dividend gives a quotient of **+1**. The remainder claim is correct. As written, the text states a general property of the routine that is false for half of all dividends.
**Fix:** "… with a divisor of 0 the unsigned division inside it produces all 1 bits; for a non-negative dividend such as 7 that is a quotient of −1, while for a negative dividend the final sign correction turns it into +1. The remainder equals the dividend in both cases." In line 416, write "On the AVR it quietly returns a wrong value (−1 for 7 / 0)".

**E4. Lines 312 and 530. Precision about the standard; contradicts line 57. Severity: high (this is the section's central "why" argument, and the reader will memorise it).**
**Problem:** "'implementation-defined' requires a *result*: the program must continue with some documented value. For division by zero, the x86's natural behavior is to stop the program, which no 'result' describes." Self-check 10 repeats it: "Implementation-defined behavior requires a documented result, which cannot describe 'the program stops'".
**Evidence:** The section's own line 57 cites C23 §6.3.1.3p3, where the implementation-defined outcome is "a result *or* an implementation-defined signal". So the standard already uses implementation-defined behavior to allow a stop. `<signal.h>` even defines SIGFPE as an erroneous arithmetic operation, "such as zero divide" (§7.14). The committee could have written "the result is implementation-defined, or an implementation-defined signal is raised". The real costs of that choice are different ones:
- Every implementation would have to choose and document an outcome, and then deliver it at exactly that point of the program. Making the trap or the value part of the program's defined behavior forbids the compiler from assuming the divisor is non-zero, and from moving, merging or deleting divisions.
- The AVR's outcome depends on the sign of the dividend (see E3), so it would have to be documented as such.
- Clang's 64-bit shift (line 306) would be forbidden.
**Fix (line 312):** "It would seem kinder to say 'implementation-defined', so that each compiler had to document an answer. The standard could even do it: for out-of-range conversions it already allows 'an implementation-defined result or an implementation-defined signal'. But a documented outcome binds the compiler. It must produce exactly that outcome, at that point in the program, every time. It could no longer assume that a divisor is non-zero or that a shift count is in range, and Clang's 64-bit shift trick above would be forbidden. 'Undefined' was the only category that let every machine, and every optimizer, keep its natural behavior." **Fix (line 530):** "… Implementation-defined behavior would oblige each compiler to document an outcome (a value or a signal) and then always produce it, which forbids optimizations that assume the divisor is non-zero; and making every division test its divisor would slow every correct program."

**E5. Line 319. Imprecise engineering reasoning (wrong example). Severity: medium-high.**
**Problem:** "A compiler that may assume `i + 1` never overflows can conclude that a loop `for (int i = 0; i < n; ++i)` runs exactly `n` times …"
**Evidence:** For this loop, the conclusion does not need the assumption. Since `i < n <= INT_MAX`, `i + 1` cannot overflow under any semantics, wrapping included. Test file /tmp/r0204/w/loop.c contains `long s=0; for(int i=0;i<n;++i) s+=a[i];`. `gcc-14 -O2` and `gcc-14 -O2 -fwrapv` produce **byte-identical** code for it (diff empty). The undefined-overflow assumption pays off for `i <= n` (with wrapping the loop is infinite when `n == INT_MAX`) and for strides such as `i += 2`. For those, the -fwrapv code differs: `sum_le` grows from 19 to 21 lines and keeps a 32-bit counter with `movslq` on every pass; `sum_st` grows from 20 to 21.
**Fix:** "… applies to the counters of loops. In `for (int i = 0; i <= n; ++i)`, if `i + 1` were allowed to wrap, the loop would never end when `n` is `INT_MAX`. A compiler that may assume signed overflow never happens can conclude that the loop runs exactly `n + 1` times and that `i` only grows, which lets it keep `i` as a 64-bit pointer offset, unroll the loop, or process several elements at once. (For the more common `i < n`, no assumption is needed: `i < n` already guarantees that `i + 1` fits.)"

**E6. Line 403. Contradiction with line 372. Severity: low-medium.**
**Problem:** "Undefined behavior appears in none of these as a promise, except where an implementation has chosen to extend the language, and then only in the description of an option such as `-fwrapv`."
**Evidence:** Line 372 (correctly) says that GCC's manual promises a definition of signed `<<` with no option at all. That promise is on the "Integers" page of "C Implementation-Defined Behavior": "As an extension to the C language, GCC does not use the latitude given in C99 and later to treat certain aspects of signed '<<' as undefined."
**Fix:** "… except where an implementation has chosen to extend the language: then it says so in its manual, either in the description of an option such as `-fwrapv` or as a documented extension, such as GCC's treatment of signed `<<`."

**E7. Line 303 (Figure 02.04-2 caption). Figure/text mismatch. Severity: low.**
**Problem:** "One shift, three machines' ideas of what it means"
**Evidence:** The figure shows two machines (x86-64 twice, AVR once) and three implementations. The Clang row exists precisely to show that the *compiler*, not the machine, produced the third answer.
**Fix:** "Figure 02.04-2  One shift, three implementations' ideas of what it means".

## 2. Likely errors

**L1. Line 306 (and Self-check 8). Instruction-level claim. Severity: medium.**
**Problem:** "it performed the shift in a 64-bit register (`shlq`). For every shift count the standard allows (0 to 31), that gives exactly the right answer and saves an instruction"
**Evidence:** On x86-64, a 32-bit `shll %cl, %edx` already clears bits 32–63 of `%rdx`, so the 32-bit version also needs exactly one instruction. GCC's own build of the same line is `movl $1,%edx; sall %cl,%edx`, the same count as Clang's `movl $1,%edx; shlq %cl,%rdx`. When the cast is changed to `(unsigned)` (/tmp/r0204/w/sh32.c), Clang emits a single `shll`. `clang -O2 -emit-llvm` shows how the 64-bit shift arose: the optimizer marked the shift `shl nuw i32 1, %n` ("no unsigned wrap"). That is legal only because a count of 32 or more is undefined. The back end then merged `zext(shl nuw …)` into one 64-bit shift. Nothing is saved; it is a rewrite that is valid for every defined count.
**Fix:** "Clang at `-O2` did something different: knowing that a count of 32 or more is undefined, it concluded that shifting 1 can never push a bit out of the 32-bit value. The result is converted to the 64-bit `unsigned long` anyway, so it performed the shift directly in a 64-bit register (`shlq`). For every count the standard allows (0 to 31), that gives exactly the right answer; for the count 32 the 1 landed at bit 32." Make the same change in the Self-check 8 answer.

**L2. Line 317. Overstatement. Severity: low-medium.**
**Problem:** "(all three of your machines use it, and so does every C implementation still maintained)"
**Evidence:** WG14 N2218, the survey that preceded N2412, found one family of machines still using ones' complement: Unisys ClearPath (the descendants of the UNIVAC 1100/2200), which Unisys still sells and emulates for legacy customers, and which has a C compiler. That compiler does not conform to recent standards. The committee's position was that no *modern* implementation needs another representation, not that none is maintained.
**Fix:** "(all three of your machines use it, and so does essentially every C implementation in current use; the survey behind the change found only one legacy mainframe family that did not)".

**L3. Lines 41 and 398. Precision about Annex J. Severity: medium.**
**Problem:** Line 41 says Annex J "lists their instances"; line 398 calls it "the checklist of everything the standard leaves open".
**Evidence:** Annex J is informative, and its own notes call it an "overview" (N3096 §3.4.1–3.4.4, Note 1 or 2: "J.x gives an overview …"). C23 §4p2 says undefined behavior is indicated in three ways: by violating a "shall" outside a constraint, by the explicit words "undefined behavior", or "by the omission of any explicit definition of behavior". The third kind cannot be listed completely. A reader told that J.2 is "everything" may conclude that anything not listed is defined.
**Fix (line 398):** "… It is a useful checklist, organized by topic, but only a summary: the binding text is in the clauses, and the standard also makes behavior undefined simply by not defining it, which no list can capture completely." Also, at line 41, "and summarizes them in its informative Annex J".

**L4. Line 317. Inference stated as the sole cause. Severity: low-medium.**
**Problem:** "It remains undefined, because over the decades compilers have come to *depend* on that freedom."
**Evidence:** The two's-complement papers (P0907R4 §2.4, the C++ twin of N2412) record three reasons for keeping overflow undefined: optimizers' assumptions, leaving room for tools such as sanitizers to treat overflow as an error, and data showing that the great majority of overflows are bugs that wrapping would not fix. The text gives only the first. It is also unsourced: the Sources list does not cite N2412's or P0907's discussion.
**Fix:** "It remains undefined, for two reasons. Compilers have come to depend on that freedom; and an overflow is almost always a bug, which tools can report as an error only while it is not given a defined meaning (a sanitizer, below, does exactly that)." Add P0907R4 §2.4 / N2412 to the Sources.

## 3. Improvements

**I1. Line 27. Completeness of "in full" (promise §02.02 → §02.04). Severity: medium.** "For every construct, it says one of five things" leaves out the standard's other tool, **constraints**: a constraint violation must be diagnosed (§5.1.1.3) and is neither of the five kinds. It also never says how undefined behavior is indicated (§4p2: "shall" outside a constraint, explicit words, or omission). Add after Table 02.04-1: "Two more rules complete the picture. Some requirements are *constraints*: breaking one (assigning a string to an `int`, say) obliges the compiler to issue a diagnostic. And undefined behavior is indicated in three ways: by the words 'undefined behavior', by breaking a 'shall' that is not a constraint, or simply by the absence of any definition (C23, §4)."

**I2. Back-reference from §02.03, line 76. Severity: low-medium.** §02.03 says what counts as an access to a `volatile` object is implementation-defined and that the compiler's documentation gives it, "as §02.04 explains". §02.04 never mentions it. In item 2 at line 399, add: "Its page 'Qualifiers' answers a question §02.03 left open: what counts as an access to a `volatile` object."

**I3. Line 383 (and Sources line 554). Tree-dump account. Severity: low.** The account is right in substance. Tested: einline shows `r.readings[i_2] = 7;`, fre1 shows `__printf_chk (2, "checksum = %d\n", 1234)`, the final code is `movl $1234,%esi`, and with `-fno-tree-sra`, fre1 alone does the substitution. However, at default `-O2` the first pass to use the in-bounds assumption is early SRA (`-fdump-tree-esra`). It splits `r.checksum` into a separate scalar `r$checksum` because `r.readings[i_2]` is taken to cover only bytes 0–15; fre1 then propagates the 1234. Suggested wording: "(…`-fdump-tree-esra` and `-fdump-tree-fre1`; the internal details below are simplified from them)".

**I4. Line 388. Count wording.** "All three outputs are permitted" — the three builds produce two distinct outputs (7 and 1234). Use "All three builds' outputs are permitted."

**I5. Line 362. Overstatement.** "avr-gcc keeps null checks always". It still deletes *redundant* checks: in /tmp/r0204/w/nc2.c, `g()` tests `p == NULL` twice and the AVR code keeps only one `sbiw/breq`. Use: "avr-gcc never deletes a null check on the grounds that the pointer was used before it, even if you ask it to with `-fdelete-null-pointer-checks`". Note: `[disabled]` was confirmed with `-Q --help=optimizers`, and the code is unchanged with the option. The 2007 patch (A. Sokolov, 30 Mar 2007, reason "The AVR HW does not implement null-pointer-checks") and the 2014 comment patch (V. K S, Atmel) were confirmed.

**I6. Line 306. Deeper AVR detail.** "executed `n` times" holds only for small `n`. The loop counts with `dec r20; brpl` on the *low byte* of `n`. Tested: counts 40, 127, 128 and 288 give 0, while 200 and 256 give 1. So the AVR also "masks" the count, to 8 bits and with a sign test, which supports the section's argument that the machines disagree. Suggested: "executed once per unit of the count's low byte (for counts below 128)".

**I7. Line 398 and Sources line 556. J.2 count.** N3096's J.2 is numbered (1) to (218). "Roughly two hundred" should become "more than two hundred (218 numbered entries in N3096)". The sources' remark that "C23's list is numbered differently" should be removed or corrected.

**I8. Line 153. Conformance precision.** "A small embedded library is entitled to support only the 'C' locale" is true, but a *hosted* implementation must still provide `setlocale`. §02.02 told the reader that avr-gcc reports itself as hosted, so missing `setlocale` is a conformance gap, not an entitlement. Add: "…and this one, although avr-gcc calls itself hosted (§02.02), does not even provide the function, a gap in its conformance." Also, the linker actually prints ``undefined reference to `setlocale'`` (with a backtick), not `'setlocale'`.

**I9. Line 450, Self-check 12(e).** `(signed char)200` is implementation-defined only if `SCHAR_MAX < 200`. Change the premise to "(on an implementation whose `char` has 8 bits and whose `int` has 32 bits)".

**I10. Line 424. Term definition.** C23 §3.4.4 also covers behavior "that results from the use of an unspecified value". Add that clause to the Terms entry, since the `:::precise` box introduces unspecified values.

**I11. Line 230.** "a program killed by the processor" should be "a program killed by the operating system after a processor exception" (matches line 306).

**I12. Line 366.** "would cost as much again" is vague, and it understates the cost of checking for dangling pointers and data races. Use "would cost more, some of them (dangling pointers, data races) much more".

**I13. Lines 12 and 388. Forward precision.** The C2y draft N3220 (§3.5.3, Note 3) adds that observable behavior which happens *before* an operation with undefined behavior must appear as specified. N3096 (C23) does not have this note. One sentence in the `:::history` box would stop "has no meaning" from being read as retroactive: "C2y's draft now states that output produced before the undefined operation must still appear."

**I14. Line 391. History overstatement.** "the definition of the language was in practice what Ritchie's compiler on the PDP-11 did". By 1978 Johnson's Portable C Compiler was in wide use, and K&R's Appendix A was a written reference manual. Use: "in practice the definition was the book's reference manual together with what the Bell Labs compilers (Ritchie's PDP-11 compiler and Johnson's portable compiler) did".

**I15. Line 410.** Fixed-width types "remove the question" of width, but not of promotion. For example, `uint16_t` × `uint16_t` is promoted to a 32-bit `int` on the laptop, can overflow there (undefined), and cannot on the AVR. Add: "(they fix the width, though not the promotions of Chapter 10)".

**I16. Line 226. Version scope.** `return x + 1 > x;` at `-O0` prints `signed: 1` with GCC 14 and 13, but `signed: 0` with avr-gcc 7.3: its `-fdump-tree-original` keeps `return x + 1 > x;`. Since the section uses both compilers, write "GCC 14 returns 1 even at `-O0` (avr-gcc 7.3 does not; the folding is version-dependent)".

**I17. Line 57. Term before definition.** "raises an implementation-defined signal" is used before signals are introduced (line 306). Add a gloss: "(a signal is a notification that, by default, ends the program; SIGFPE below is one)".

**I18. Line 508. Unrun section stated as fact.** "Notice that the line `dividing 7 by 0 ...` does appear before the crash" should be "should appear".

## Disposition (author, 26 Nov 2026)

Every finding was checked before it was applied.
- **Reproduced in the workspace:** E3 (−7/0 gives +1 on the simulator), E5 (GCC 14 at -O2: the `i < n` loop is identical with and without `-fwrapv`, and the `i <= n` loop differs), L1 (Clang's LLVM IR shows `shl nuw i32` followed by `zext`), and I6 (counts 200 and 256 give 1 on the AVR).
- **Confirmed against sources:** E1 against §02.02's text; I7 (218 numbered J.2 entries in N3096) from the draft itself; L4 from P0907, whose reasons are performance, leeway for sanitizers, and Google's data that most overflow is a bug.

**Applied as proposed or with light rewording:**
- Errors: E1, E2, E3, E4, E5, E6, E7.
- Likely errors: L1, L3, L4.
- Improvements: I1, I2, I3, I4, I5, I6, I7, I8, I9, I10, I11, I12, I14, I15, I16, I17, I18.

**Applied with changes:**
- **L2.** The text now says "as do nearly all C implementations in use today". The N2218 survey itself was not consulted by the author, so it is not cited.
- **I8.** The linker message is paraphrased ("an undefined reference to `setlocale`") rather than quoted, which avoids the backtick.

**Not applied:**
- **I13.** The C2y note on observable behavior before an undefined operation. It was not verified by the author, and it lies beyond C23, the book's baseline. It is worth revisiting in §34.01.
