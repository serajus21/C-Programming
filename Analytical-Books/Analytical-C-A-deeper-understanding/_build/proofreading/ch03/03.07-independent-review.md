# Independent review of §03.07 "Loading and Running"

Reviewer's basis: 03.07.md, out.pdf (32 pages), logs 087 to 091, gdb.out, count.gdb, avr_life.elf, prev-03.06.md. I checked glibc source from the bminor/glibc master mirror: `sysdeps/x86_64/start.S`, `csu/libc-start.c`, `elf/dl-lookup.c`, `elf/rtld.c`. I also ran tests in the Ubuntu 24.04 workspace (gcc-14, glibc 2.39, gdb, simavr, avr-gcc 7.3). Each result below says which machine it comes from.

## Summary

This is a strong section. Every man-page quotation matches log 091 word for word. Every `start.S` comment quoted in Table 03.07-1 and in the "Three details" list matches the source. All of these check out:

- The `strace` line counts (34 calls in all; the first 28; the last 6) and the line-number groupings.
- The auxv type numbers and the stack arithmetic (`argv` + 32 bytes, the `and`/`push` sequence 0x…e3a8 → e3a0 → e390, and the pushed value 0x…e398 next to 0x38).
- The PIE base subtraction.
- The libc base + offset sums, 0x7ffff7da5000 + 0x7d390 and + 0xd9a60, which are arithmetically right.
- Every AVR figure: 38 instructions = 1+6+19+11+1; cycles 9+28+14+4 = 55, which is 3.44 µs; 9 instructions after `main`; and Self-check 12's +490 instructions and +882 cycles.
- Self-check 3 (0x400318).

I re-ran the AVR count in the workspace and got 38, so the instruction count is reproduced as well as computed.

The errors fall into five groups:
1. **Technical facts.**
   - The `rjmp` loop runs 8 million times a second, not 16 million.
   - `LD_BIND_NOW` does not make `.got.plt` read-only.
   - `.init9` jumps to `exit`, not `_exit`.
   - `nm` values are not "counted from the start of the file".
   - The "number of relocations" statistic counts symbol lookups.
   - `ld.so.cache` is not built only from the directories in `/etc/ld.so.conf`.
2. **A misread backtrace.** 0x401075 is exactly `order`'s `hlt`. The text compares it with `hello`'s layout, and the "not examined" remark is unnecessary.
3. **Cross-references and evidence that do not exist.**
   - §03.06 does not give 0x7d390 or 0xd9a60.
   - §03.06 does not record the linker "removing the property".
   - No log shows a 2.4 GHz clock.
   - No log runs `where_pie` under `gdb`.
4. **Output fidelity.** Several outputs omit lines without saying so, including the whole `avr-gdb` stepping trace. The `count.gdb` shown cannot produce the output printed in Step 7. The "last lines" of the bindings trace are not the last lines.
5. **Answers, labels and layout.**
   - Experiment 03.07-3 asks something the listing cannot show, and its answer skips it.
   - Several items that were not run are not labeled as such.
   - Five `####` headings render literally in the PDF.
   - Wide `info proc mappings` lines wrap and split `r--p` into `r--` / `p`.

On depth: the biggest gap is *why* the loader fills `__libc_start_main`'s slot eagerly and `puts`'s lazily. `start.S` itself answers this in a sentence the section cut off. The second gap is that two LD_DEBUG lines the text treats as the loader's trace ("initialize program", "transferring control") are actually printed by `__libc_start_main`. That fact, and the `init == NULL` branch beside it, would turn part of the "GLIBC_2.34" inference into a sourced fact.

Counts: **Errors 22, Improvements 16, Nits 12.**

## Errors

### E1. The AVR stop loop runs 8 million times a second, not 16 million
- **Text (Step 7):** "The processor executes a jump to itself, 16 million times a second, with interrupts disabled, until a reset."
- **Wrong:** `rjmp` takes 2 cycles. The section itself says so ("rjmp 2" in Sources; the 55-cycle total counts `rjmp` as 2). At 16 MHz that is 8,000,000 executions a second.
- **Correction:** "The processor executes a jump to itself, 8 million times a second (one `rjmp` every 2 of its 16 million cycles), with interrupts disabled, until a reset."

### E2. `LD_BIND_NOW` does not let the whole table be protected
- **Text (RELRO):** "(With `LD_BIND_NOW` and the linker option `-z now`, every slot is filled at start-up and the whole table can be protected; §34.06 compares the two.)"
- **Wrong:** The RELRO range is fixed in the file by the linker. Here `GNU_RELRO` ends at 0x403000, before `.got.plt`. `LD_BIND_NOW` changes *when* slots are filled, but not *what* `mprotect` covers. Only linking with `-z now` (together with `-z relro`) makes the linker move `.got.plt` inside `GNU_RELRO` (full RELRO). As written, "and" reads as if either one, or the pair, protects the table.
- **Evidence (Ubuntu workspace):** I built a lazy non-PIE program (`-no-pie -Wl,-z,lazy`) that prints its own `/proc/self/maps`. With and without `LD_BIND_NOW=1`, the page holding `.got.plt` (0x404000) stays `rw-p`; only the RELRO page 0x403000 becomes `r--p`.
- **Correction:** "(`LD_BIND_NOW` fills every slot at start-up but leaves `.got.plt` writable, because the read-only range was fixed by the linker. Linking with `-z now` both fills every slot at start-up and lets the linker place `.got.plt` inside `GNU_RELRO`, so that the whole table is protected; §34.06 compares the two.)"

### E3. The frame at 0x401075 is `order`'s `hlt`, not an address "past `_start`"
- **Text (Step 4, item 1):** "(The address 0x401075 in frame `_start` lies past `_start`'s last instruction, the `hlt` at 0x401065: `gdb` shows return addresses, and its name for this frame is its best guess. The author has not examined why the address is 0x401075 rather than 0x401065; the frame is `_start`'s.)"
- **Wrong:** 0x401065 is the `hlt` of **`hello`**. The backtrace is of **`order`**, which imports two functions (`puts` and `__cxa_atexit`; `atexit` comes from `libc_nonshared.a`). Its PLT therefore has two entries, `.text` starts at 0x401050 (as for `stack` and `where`), and the `hlt` is at 0x401050 + 0x25 = 0x401075. The breakpoint at `before` (0x40114a = 0x401146 + 4, after `push %rbp; mov %rsp,%rbp`) is consistent with that layout. So 0x401075 is exactly the return address of the `call`, i.e. the `hlt`, and `gdb`'s naming is correct, not a guess.
- **Evidence (Ubuntu workspace):** `order.c` built as on Fedora (`-no-pie -fno-pie -fcf-protection=none -Wl,-z,lazy`) gives `_start` at 0x401050, `call *…` at 0x40106f, `hlt` at 0x401075, and `#7 0x0000000000401075 in _start ()` in the backtrace at `after`.
- **Correction:** "(0x401075 is the return address of `_start`'s `call`, which is its `hlt`: in `order`, which imports two functions instead of one, the PLT is 16 bytes longer, so `_start` begins at 0x401050 instead of 0x401040, and its `hlt` is at 0x401050 + 0x25 = 0x401075. `gdb` always shows the caller's frame at its return address.)" Also remove "the author has not examined…".

### E4. §03.06 does not give the `nm -D` values 0x7d390 and 0xd9a60, and no log provided does
- **Text (Step 2):** "`nm -D /lib64/libc.so.6` in §03.06 gave `__libc_start_main@@GLIBC_2.34` the value 0x7d390" and "`nm -D` gave `puts@@GLIBC_2.2.5` the value 0xd9a60".

### E5. `nm` values are offsets from the library's base address, not from "the start of the library file"
- **Text:** "the value 0x7d390, an address counted from the start of the library file"
- **Wrong:** A symbol's value in a shared library is a *virtual address relative to the load base*, the address where the library's first segment lands. It equals a file offset only where the two happen to coincide, which they do for libc's text but not for its data. Log 089 shows libc's `r--p` data mapping at 0x7ffff7f95000 with file offset 0x1ef000, which is base + 0x1f0000. Line 16 of the `strace` maps file offset 0x1ef000 at base + 0x1f0000 (0x7f8c49ef4000 − 0x7f8c49d04000). A reader who takes "counted from the start of the file" literally will add bases to file offsets and get data addresses wrong by a page.
- **Correction:** "an address counted from the library's *base*, the place where its first byte is loaded (for code, this happens to equal the position in the file; for data it does not, as the mapping at offset 0x1ef000 placed at base + 0x1f0000 shows)".

### E6. The "number of relocations" statistic counts symbol lookups, and "relative relocations" does not count libc's
- **Text:** "96 relocations at start-up, most of them inside `libc.so.6` itself" and, in Step 5, "These are the *relative relocations* that `LD_DEBUG=statistics` counted as 0 for `hello`."
- **Wrong:**
  - In glibc, `number of relocations` is `GL(dl_num_relocations)`. It is incremented once per call to `_dl_lookup_symbol_x` (`elf/dl-lookup.c`: `bump_num_relocations ();`, line 773 on master). So it counts *symbol lookups*, not relocations applied. libc.so.6 applies far more than 96 relocations.
  - `number of relative relocations` is the sum of `DT_RELACOUNT` over the loaded objects (`elf/rtld.c`, `print_statistics`). It does not count relative relocations packed as `DT_RELR`.
  - libc.so.6 is itself loaded at a random base and certainly has relative relocations, yet the count is 0. A first-principles reader will conclude that libc needs none.
- **Evidence:**
  - The bindings trace in log 088 has 95 `binding` lines before "transferring control" and 1 after it (for `puts`), matching 96 → 97 lookups. (This also *supports* the author's inference about the 97th.)
  - Ubuntu workspace: `readelf -d libc.so.6` shows `RELR` and `RELRSZ`, and `readelf -r` finds 0 `RELATIVE` entries. `LD_DEBUG=statistics` reports 0 relative relocations for a non-PIE hello and 3 for a PIE hello.
- **Correction:**
  - Step 2: "96 symbol lookups at start-up (the statistic is named 'relocations', but glibc increments it once per name looked up), most of them by `libc.so.6` itself".
  - Step 5: "…must be adjusted by the base. `LD_DEBUG=statistics` counts these *relative relocations* only where an object lists them as ordinary relocations (`DT_RELACOUNT`). `hello` has none. `libc.so.6` has many, but stores them in a packed form (`DT_RELR`) that this counter does not include, which is why it printed 0." If the numbers are wanted for Fedora, add `readelf -d /lib64/libc.so.6 | grep RELR` and `LD_DEBUG=statistics ./where_pie` to the jobs.

## Improvements

### I1. Explain why `__libc_start_main`'s slot is filled eagerly and `puts`'s lazily, using `start.S`'s own words
The section shows the fact ("The loader filled the slot of `__libc_start_main` and left the slot of `puts` alone") but never gives the mechanism. That gap leaves lazy binding as half a black box. The mechanism:
- `R_X86_64_GLOB_DAT` names a slot that code reads *directly* (`call *0x1f73(%rip)`), with no fallback path. It must hold the real address before the code runs.
- `R_X86_64_JUMP_SLOT` names a slot that initially points back into the PLT stub, which leads to the resolver. That fallback is what makes laziness possible.

The Table's quotation of the `call` comment stops just before the sentence that says this: "Since __libc_start_main in libc.so is called very early, lazy binding isn't relevant here. Use indirect branch via GOT to avoid extra branch to PLT slot." Quote it and connect it to the two relocation types.

### I2. "initialize program" and "transferring control" are printed by `__libc_start_main`, not by the loader's jump
In `csu/libc-start.c` (master), these are `GLRO(dl_debug_printf) ("\ninitialize program: %s\n\n", argv[0]);` (line 338) and `("\ntransferring control: %s\n\n", argv[0]);` (line 353). Both run inside `__libc_start_main`, *after* the loader has already jumped to `_start`.

### I3. Use `libc-start.c` to turn part of the GLIBC_2.34 inference into a sourced fact, and to open "finds the constructors itself"
The same source shows `if (init != NULL) /* This is a legacy program which supplied its own init routine. */ … else /* This is a current program. Use the dynamic segment to find constructors. */ call_init (…)`.

### I5. The loader that relocates everything must first relocate itself
`AT_BASE` is introduced, but not why ld.so needs it. ld.so is a shared object placed at a random address. Its first job (`_dl_start`) is to apply its own relative relocations using `AT_BASE`, before it can use any global data or call through any table. After that it loads libraries, relocates them, runs `_dl_init`, and jumps to `AT_ENTRY`. Without this, "who relocates the relocator?" is the obvious unanswered question.

### I6. Name the kernel's ELF loader and complete its list of work
For a first-principles reader, add that `execve` dispatches to the kernel's ELF handler (`load_elf_binary` in `fs/binfmt_elf.c`). Besides the three listed actions, the kernel also:
- Zero-fills the tail of the last page of the data segment and maps extra zero pages when `MemSiz > FileSiz`. This is `.bss`: `hello`'s 4 bytes, and the anonymous `rw-p` page at 0x7ffff7ffe000 for the loader.
- Sets the initial program break (`brk`).
- Maps the `[vdso]`/`[vvar]`, which the bullet list attributes to the kernel but the numbered list omits.

Also mention the point of no return: after a certain moment a failed `execve` cannot return to the old program.

### I7. Account for the anonymous `rw-p` line at 0x7ffff7ffe000
The text claims "Every line can be traced to §03.06", but this line is never mentioned. It is the loader's own zero-filled data. That one address ties Step 1, Step 2 and Step 4 together.

### I8. Why function-pointer comparison works across the whole process (the canonical PLT address)
"Every comparison of function pointers still works, because every part of the program uses the same value" understates the mechanism, because libraries also take function addresses. The full story:
- When a non-PIE program takes `&puts`, the linker gives the executable's dynamic symbol `puts` a non-zero value, the PLT address.
- The loader then resolves *every* address-of reference to `puts` in the process, including any inside libc, to 0x401030.

That is why the PLT entry is the canonical address process-wide. Label it as the author's understanding if it is not demonstrated (`readelf --dyn-syms where | grep puts` would show the non-zero value).

### I10. Name what `_xsavec` in the resolver's name means
The resolver must not disturb the arguments already in registers for `puts`, including vector registers. `_dl_runtime_resolve_xsavec` saves them with the `XSAVEC` instruction before calling the lookup and restores them afterwards. One sentence removes the black box.
