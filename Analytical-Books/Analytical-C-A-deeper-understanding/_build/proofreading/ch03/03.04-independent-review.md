# Independent review of §03.04 "Compiling: From C to Assembly"

Reviewer's material: 03.04.md (846 lines), 03.04-rendered.pdf (34 pages), fig_cc1.png, the program sources, logs 057–063, source-notes.md, and the approved sections 03.01–03.03.
Reviewer's own shell (for reasoning only; the Fedora logs are the authority): Ubuntu 24.04, gcc 13.3 / gcc-14.2, binutils 2.42, avr-gcc 7.3.0 with binutils 2.26.
Line numbers below refer to 03.04.md.

## Verdict

**Revise before approval.** The section is strong. It is well built, close to the benchmarks in depth, and nearly all of its evidence matches the logs: every x86 listing, the 21 and 30 byte counts, the call-displacement arithmetic, the 27→19 line count, the 54 dump files, the 51/93/144/157/135/81 option counts, the 648,574 inputs, the fingerprint, the check10 and divcost outputs, the AVR listings, and the 197–213 cycle count. The proof for 0xCCCCCCCD is correct, and the quotations from the GCC manual, the Intel SDM, Csmith and CompCert check out against their sources.

Some errors still have to be fixed, though:

- One table column measures the wrong thing (`size` counts `.eh_frame`).
- One headline claim is not backed by the logs ("all six give the same results").
- The seven instructions of `main` are attributed to the wrong lines of source, and that paragraph is the very promise §03.01 made.
- The listings are numbered out of order.
- Three claims are contradicted by the section's own evidence or its sources: the AVR "cheaper" claim, the DO-178C reading, and "virtual registers".
- Several excerpts are shortened without saying so.

None of these needs new structure. All of them can be fixed in place, with the few Fedora runs listed at the end.

## ERRORS (must fix)

### E1. Table 03.04-3 and the opening puzzle report `size`'s "text" total as "code bytes", but that total includes `.eh_frame` and a note section
Exact text:
- Line 15: "produce six different pieces of machine code, from 172 to 358 bytes"
- Line 452: column heading "Code bytes (`size`)", with values 265 / 203 / 237 / 358 / 172 / 196.

Why it is wrong: Berkeley-format `size` (the default) adds every allocated read-only section to its "text" column. §03.01 itself says this column counts "code and read-only data". The objects in log 057 were built **without** `-fno-asynchronous-unwind-tables`, so the total includes `.eh_frame` (88–120 bytes for these three functions).

In the reviewer's shell, gcc-14 with `-fcf-protection=none` gives these totals: -O0 217 = `.text` 97 + `.eh_frame` 120; -O1 155 = 67 + 88; -O2 189 = 101 + 88; -O3 308 = 204 + `.rodata.cst16` 16 + 88; -Os 124 = 36 + 88; -Og 148 = 60 + 88. Each of the reader's totals is exactly 48 bytes more than these for five of the six levels (265, 203, 237, 172, 196). That almost certainly means a 48-byte `.note.gnu.property` section on Fedora plus identical code. So the real machine code on Fedora is probably about **36 to 206 bytes**, not 172 to 358, and at -Os code is only about a fifth of the figure printed.

The cross-check is direct: log 058 shows `div10` at -Os occupying offsets 0x4–0xf, and the whole -Os `.text` is about 36 bytes, not 172.

Correction: measure `.text` (and `.rodata*`) separately. Use `size -A` and relabel the column "`.text` bytes". If you want to keep the totals, rename the column "`size` text (code + read-only data + unwind tables)". Then fix line 15 and the myth box to match. "-O3 made the biggest code" stays true.

Fedora command:
```
for O in O0 O1 O2 O3 Os Og; do gcc -std=c23 -$O -c ops.c -o ops_$O.o; echo "-- $O"; size -A ops_$O.o | grep -E '^\.(text|rodata|eh_frame|note)'; done
```

### E3. The seven instructions are attributed wrongly, and the prologue/epilogue are said to be required by the convention
Exact text (line 170): "Only one of the seven instructions comes from the statement `printf("hello, world\n");`, with its argument in `edi`; one comes from `return 0;`; the other five are the function's **prologue** and **epilogue**, the fixed preparation and cleanup that the calling convention requires around any function."

Why it is wrong:
- **The split is wrong.** Two instructions come from the `printf` statement: `mov $0x4004b0,%edi` and `call`. Log 057's `-fverbose-asm` output puts both under `# hello.c:6: printf(...)`. The prologue and epilogue are four (`push`, `mov`, `pop`, `ret`), not five.
- **The convention requires no such code.** The System V ABI does not require these instructions. It requires only that `rbp` (and the other callee-saved registers) be returned unchanged and that the stack be aligned at calls. The `push %rbp; mov %rsp,%rbp` frame is GCC's choice at -O0, and the section itself shows it disappearing at -O2 (lines 258–267).

Correction: "Two of the seven come from `printf("hello, world\n");`: one places the argument in `edi`, one calls. One comes from `return 0;`. The other four are the function's **prologue** and **epilogue**. Here that means code GCC at -O0 places around every function to build and dismantle a frame. The convention itself demands only that `rbp` come back unchanged and that the stack be aligned at a call, and at -O2 these instructions disappear (below)."

### E5. The size of the 64-bit form is explained inconsistently: "an extra prefix byte" (line 138) against "two bytes shorter" (line 164)
Line 138 says that the 64-bit forms "need an extra prefix byte, as you will see in a moment". Line 164 then says that `movl $imm32,%edi` "is two bytes shorter than its 64-bit form". Both cannot be the whole story, and a careful reader will notice the mismatch.

The facts, assembled in the reviewer's shell:

| Instruction | Bytes | Encoding |
|---|---|---|
| `movl $0x4004b0,%edi` | 5 | `bf b0 04 40 00` |
| `movq $0x4004b0,%rdi` | 7 | `48 c7 c7 b0 04 40 00` |
| `movabs $0x4004b0,%rdi` | 10 | `48 bf b0 04 40 00 00 00 00 00` |

With REX.W, the short "B8+r" opcode takes a **64-bit** immediate. A 64-bit move of a 32-bit constant must therefore use the C7 /0 form, which needs a ModRM byte as well as the REX prefix: two extra bytes.

Correction at line 138: "(the 64-bit forms need at least an extra prefix byte, as you will see in a moment)". Add to line 164: "its 64-bit form, `movq $0x4004b0,%rdi`, encodes as `48 c7 c7 b0 04 40 00`: the REX prefix plus a ModRM byte, because with REX.W the short `bf` form would take an 8-byte constant." Fedora check: `printf 'movl $0x4004b0,%%edi\nmovq $0x4004b0,%%rdi\nmovabsq $0x4004b0,%%rdi\n' | as -o t.o - && objdump -d t.o`.

### E6. avr_hello: "which is cheaper in this loop" is unsupported and, by cycle count, false
Exact text (line 679): "…and replaced a test of each character by a test of the address, which is cheaper in this loop."

The two loops cost the same per character, apart from the UART wait. avr-gcc 15 runs `ld` 2 + `sts` 2 + `ldi` 1 + `cpi` 1 + `cpc` 1 + `brne` 2 = 9 cycles. avr-gcc 7.3, compiled in the reviewer's shell, gives `ld r24,Z+; cpse r24,__zero_reg__; rjmp .L3; … sts 198,r24; rjmp .L2`, which is `ld` 2 + `cpse` 1 + `rjmp` 2 + `sts` 2 + `rjmp` 2 = 9 cycles. Both loops are 11 words of flash, counting the `rjmp .L4` that 7.3 places inside its loop. In any case, the loop is dominated by waiting for the UART, about 85 µs, or roughly 1,360 cycles, per character at this baud rate. The claim is also not listed as inference.

Correction: drop "which is cheaper in this loop". Say what can be said: GCC 15 knows the string, so it knows the trip count (14), turns the exit test into a comparison of the pointer with its final value, and can skip the test before the first character, which it knows is not zero. If a reason is given, label it as inference.

### E7. "In orbit": the DO-178C reading is contradicted by the standard's own Note, and the paraphrase drops material words
Exact text (line 682): "…requires for its highest level (Level A) that, where a compiler generates object code not directly traceable to the source code, additional verification be performed… A compiler-generated check of an array index is the standard's own example; the multiplication that replaced `x / 10`, or `__udivmodhi4` on the AVR, would be others, in the author's reading."

DO-178C §6.4.4.2.b reads, as quoted by LDRA ("Object Code Verification and Objective A7-9"): "Independent of the code form on which the structural coverage analysis is performed, if the software level is A and a compiler, linker, or other means generates additional code that is not directly traceable to Source Code statements, then additional verification should be performed to establish the correctness of such generated code sequences." Its Note defines that code as code "that introduces branches or side effects that are not immediately apparent at the Source Code level".

The multiply-and-shift sequence for `x / 10` has no branches and no side effects. By the Note's own definition it is therefore *not* an example, and "the author's reading" contradicts the source. `__udivmodhi4` is a much better candidate, because it contains a loop and conditional branches that the source does not show, although in practice library routines are often qualified separately.

Correction: remove the multiplication from the examples, and keep `__udivmodhi4` with the Note's criterion stated. Quote the clause verbatim: the reader wants exact quotations, and the words "compiler, linker, or other means", "should" and "structural coverage analysis" matter. Optionally, cite the FAA's CAST-12 position paper, "Guidelines for Approving Source Code to Object Code Traceability" (2002), which describes acceptable methods.

### E8. The main-text argument for M − 1 is incomplete, so it does not yet predict "exactly" 429,496,729
Line 497 shows only that the positive multiples of 10 fail. To predict the count *exactly*, as line 539 claims ("exactly as the arithmetic predicted"), the text must also show that no other `x` fails. That half appears only in the answer to Self-check 11. Similarly, for M, line 495 covers only the case "when it is not a whole number". The whole-number case is trivially fine, but it is not stated.

The reviewer redid the math, and it holds. Write x = 10q + r with 0 ≤ r ≤ 9.
- **With M:** the value is q + r/10 + e, with 0 ≤ e = 0.2x/2^35 < 0.025. So q ≤ value < q + 0.925, and the floor is q for every x.
- **With M − 1:** the value is q + r/10 − d, with d = 0.8x/2^35. Because x ≤ 2^32 − 1, d < 0.1 strictly, and d > 0 for x > 0.
  - If r = 0 and x > 0, the floor is q − 1, which is wrong.
  - If r ≥ 1, the value is at least q + 0.1 − d > q, so the floor is q, which is right.
  - x = 0 gives 0, which is right.

So the failures are exactly the ⌊(2^32 − 1)/10⌋ = 429,496,729 positive multiples of 10.

Correction: move the r ≥ 1 case (one sentence: "for any other x, x/10 is at least 0.1 above a whole number, and d < 0.1 cannot pull it below") into the main text, and use the q + r/10 form, which makes both cases visible at a glance.

### E9. "Virtual registers" for GCC's pseudo-registers (text and figure)
Exact text (line 337): "on an unlimited supply of 'virtual' registers … allocate real registers to the virtual ones (the pass `ira`)". Figure 03.04-1 says "virtual registers replaced by eax, edi …".

GCC's own term is **pseudo-registers** (pseudos): the `(reg:DI 101)` in the expand dump. In GCC, "virtual registers" are something different, a handful of placeholders such as virtual-stack-vars, and they are replaced by the pass `vregs`. That pass appears in the reader's own file list, right after `expand` (`hello.c.275r.vregs`). A reader who looks it up will be misled.

Correction: "an unlimited supply of *pseudo-registers* (GCC's term)", and the same change in the figure.

### E14. -O1 `sum_to`: "which needs one test instead of two" is unsupported
Exact text (line 448): "…and it compares `i` with `n + 1` for equality, which needs one test instead of two."

The original condition `i <= n` is also one compare and one branch. The -O0 loop already runs one conditional branch per iteration (`cmpl`/`jle` at `.L6`). There is no second test that disappears.

What actually happened: the loop was *rotated*. The test `n ≥ 1` is made once, before the loop, and the bottom test `i != n + 1` is equivalent to `i <= n` because `i` starts at 1 and rises by exactly 1. That equivalence uses the fact that `i` cannot overflow, since signed overflow is undefined (§02.04, §04.04), so the compiler may assume it does not happen. The -Os version, by contrast, tests at the top *and* jumps back (`jg` plus `jmp`), which is the "two" the author may have meant.

Correction: replace the clause with this account, or delete it.

### E15. Table 03.04-1's ".L names are kept out of the object file's table of names" is contradicted, unexplained, by the AVR evidence in the same section
Line 79 makes the claim without qualification. Yet the `avr-nm avr_ops.o` output on line 630 shows `0000000e t .L3`, and log 057's `avr-objdump` of the ELF shows many `.Loc.N` labels (which the Hands-on removes with `grep`).

The reviewer's avr-gcc 7.3 with binutils 2.26 does **not** keep `.L3`, which shows the behavior depends on the version.

Correction: in Table 03.04-1, write "…are normally kept out of the object file's table of names (on x86-64; the AVR toolchain keeps some, as `avr-nm` shows below)", and add one sentence at the `avr-nm` output. Fedora check: `avr-objdump -r avr_ops.o` (expect `R_AVR_13_PCREL .L3`) and `avr-gcc -mmcu=atmega328p -Os -c avr_ops.c -v 2>&1 | grep -o -- '-mlink-relax'`.

### E16. Floating-point and vector registers: wrong for the reader's own processor
Exact text (line 136): "Besides these there are 16 registers for floating-point and vector work, `xmm0` to `xmm15` (128 bits each on every x86-64 processor)".

Every x86-64 processor has at least those 16. The reader's i5-1135G7 (Tiger Lake) implements AVX-512, so in 64-bit mode it has 32 vector registers of 512 bits (`zmm0`–`zmm31`, whose low 128 bits are `xmm0`–`xmm31`) and 8 mask registers. All x86-64 processors also keep the x87 stack `st(0)`–`st(7)`, which GCC on Linux uses for `long double` (§07.07). So "there are 16 registers for floating-point" is false on the reader's machine.

Correction: "…at least 16 registers for floating-point and vector work, `xmm0` to `xmm15`, 128 bits each, which every x86-64 processor has (the reader's i5-1135G7, which has AVX-512, has 32, each the low part of a 512-bit register), and the older x87 registers, which C on Linux uses for `long double` (§07.07)". Fedora check: `grep -o -w -m1 avx512f /proc/cpuinfo`.

## IMPROVEMENTS (should do)

1. **The red zone.** At -O0, `times10` and `div10` store `x` at `-4(%rbp)` while `rbp == rsp`, that is, *below* the stack pointer, with no `subq`. The `main` of `call.c`, by contrast, reserves 16 bytes. A first-principles reader will see a store below `rsp` and ask whether it is legal. One sentence fixes this, pointing to §19.02. The psABI defines a 128-byte "red zone" below `rsp` that signal handlers may not touch, and a leaf function may use it without moving `rsp`. A function that calls another, like `main` here, may not. This is the single most likely "but why?" in the section.

2. **Why 35, and not 32 or 34.** The text says GCC rounds 2^35/10 up, but not why the shift is 35. With 34, the rounded-up constant is 1,717,986,919, the error term is 0.6x/2^34, and that can reach 0.15. Adding 0.15 to a fractional part of 0.9 crosses the next whole number. The reviewer's exhaustive test found 143,165,576 failures, the first at x = 2,863,311,539. So 35 is the smallest shift that works, and the same inequality decides it. The same arithmetic explains Experiment 03.04-2's `s10`: there |x| < 2^31, so a shift of 34 suffices. Cite the method's source: T. Granlund and P. L. Montgomery, "Division by Invariant Integers using Multiplication", PLDI 1994 (also H. Warren, *Hacker's Delight*, ch. 10). The section currently presents GCC's constant with no published origin. Fedora check: extend check10.c with a third test using 1717986919u and `>> 34`.

3. **Which pass merges the two shifts.** The -O0 code shifts by 32 and then by 3, and the expand dump at -O2 shows the same pair. At -O1 and above a single `shrq $35` appears. In the reviewer's gcc-14 dumps, the constant 35 first appears in `combine` (`ops.c.298r.combine`). `combine` is an "improving" RTL pass that does not run at -O0, which is exactly the section's own lesson. Add one sentence. Fedora check: `gcc -O2 -fdump-rtl-all -S ops.c && grep -l 'const_int 35' ops.c.*r.*`.

4. **Where the -O2 unrolling comes from.** On x86 since GCC 13, the back end enables RTL unrolling of small loops at -O2 (`-munroll-only-small-loops`). In the reviewer's gcc-14, the tree dump `optimized` still shows the simple loop, and the RTL pass `loop2_unroll` reports "Unrolled loop 1 times". That is also the missing answer to Experiment 03.04-3 ("is it already unrolled?" No). Without this, Table 03.04-3 suggests "-O2 unrolls by two" in general. Fedora check: `gcc -O2 -Q --help=target | grep unroll`, and `gcc -O2 -fdump-rtl-loop2_unroll -S ops.c; grep -i unrolled ops.c.*loop2_unroll`.

5. **Give the documented reason for printf→puts in `lower` (verbatim, from GCC's source).** `gcc/gimple-low.cc`, `lower_stmt`, contains: "We delay folding of built calls from gimplification to here so the IL is in consistent state for the diagnostic machineries job." The transformation itself is `gimple_fold_builtin_printf` in `gcc/gimple-fold.cc`, with the comments "If the return value is used, don't do the transformation." and "If the string was "string\n", call puts("string")." This replaces "while lowering the call it recognized…" (line 312), which is slightly wrong: the pass does not lower the call, it *folds* built-in calls whose folding was postponed from gimplification. It also gives the reader the documented reason he asks for.

6. **Register allocation is two passes.** "allocate real registers … (the pass `ira`)" leaves out `reload` (330r.reload in the reader's list), which in modern GCC is LRA and completes the allocation. Say "ira and reload".

7. **Use the pass counts already measured.** Log 060 has 141 passes ON at -O0 and 281 ON (of 394) at -O2 (`-fdump-passes`). These are better than "some hundreds", which overstates the -O0 case. "-fdump-tree-all … produce one file per pass" should also read "per pass that runs and writes a dump": 141 active passes gave 54 files.

8. **Register names (the brief asked for sil/dil/bpl/spl and r8b).** The history paragraph explains `e` and `r`, but not where `sil`, `dil`, `bpl`, `spl` and `r8b`–`r15b` came from. The 8086 could address only `al`–`dl` and `ah`–`dh`. AMD64 made the low bytes of `si`, `di`, `bp`, `sp` and `r8`–`r15` addressable, and these need a REX prefix (`movb $0,%sil` is `40 b6 00`). With a REX prefix, `ah`, `bh`, `ch` and `dh` cannot be encoded, which is why the table shows no "high 8" column. Intel's SDM calls the byte registers R8L–R15L, while GNU as and AMD write r8b. Add this, and extend the REX entry in the terms list ("…or reach `r8`–`r15`, or the byte registers `sil`, `dil`, `bpl`, `spl`").

9. **DWARF register numbers in the CFI box.** `.cfi_offset 6, -16`, `.cfi_def_cfa_register 6` and `.cfi_def_cfa 7, 8` use DWARF register numbers: 6 is `rbp` and 7 is `rsp`, with 0–5 being rax, rdx, rcx, rbx, rsi, rdi. One sentence turns three opaque lines into readable ones.

10. **The GIMPLE dump's duplicated `return`.** The reader will ask why `D.2661 = 0; return D.2661;` appears twice. §03.02 already explained this (the `return 0` C adds at the closing brace of `main`), so cross-reference it.

11. **The SSA notation.** Explain `n_5(D)` (the "default definition": the parameter's value on entry) and `[INV]` (the branch probability is not yet known). Both are visible in the block, and neither is explained.

12. **RIP-relative addressing outside PIE.** In `call.c` (non-PIE), `movsd .LC0(%rip),%xmm0` is RIP-relative, while `movl $.LC1,%edi` is absolute. The reader has just learned that RIP-relative addressing is "the PIE technique". Add that, in 64-bit mode, RIP-relative addressing is the shortest way to address memory (an absolute 32-bit address needs an extra SIB byte), so GCC uses it for memory operands even when it is not required.

13. **Why the PIE code needs the 64-bit `rdi`.** Item 3 explains `edi`: "the address fits in 32 bits". The PIE version must write the full 64-bit `rdi` (`lea …,%rax; mov %rax,%rdi`) because, once the program can be loaded anywhere, the address is no longer known to fit in 32 bits. Linux places PIE programs far above 4 GiB. That sentence closes the loop.

14. **Evidence for "Fedora's GCC enables neither."** Cite all of the logged evidence:
    - `echo | gcc -dM -E -` shows no `__CET__` as well as no `__PIE__`.
    - `gcc -v` lists no `--enable-default-pie`.

    The claim that `redhat-hardened-cc1` "makes code position-independent" is correct, but it is not in the logs. Fedora check: `cat /usr/lib/rpm/redhat/redhat-hardened-cc1`.

15. **CET is enforced only when the OS turns it on.** "on a processor that enforces it" holds only if the kernel enables indirect-branch tracking for the process and every linked object is marked compatible. The reader's CPU supports CET, but that alone does not make `hello_pie` protected. "defeats attacks" should be "hinders attacks", because whole-function reuse is still possible. Fedora check: `readelf -n hello_pie | grep -i -A1 'x86 feature'`.

16. **The vectorized `sum_to` and small `n`** (myth box). The -O3 code already sends n ≤ 4 straight to the scalar tail (`leal -1(%rdi),%eax; cmpl $3,%eax; jbe .L9`), so "for small n its setup costs more than it saves" partly contradicts the listing. Rephrase: "GCC itself skips the vector path for n ≤ 4; for moderate n the setup and the final combination of the four sums may cost more than they save".

17. **AVR at -O0 also multiplies.** Log 057 shows `div10` at -O0 on the AVR using `__umulhisi3` with 0xCCCD, exactly like x86 at -O0. Saying so reinforces the section's main lesson about `expand`. Also note that the AVR object sizes (190/86/56) exclude the library routines: `__udivmodhi4` itself is 40 bytes (0x9c–0xc3 in log 058). The same proof covers 0xCCCD with shift 19: the error is below 0.2·2^16/2^19 = 0.025, and the reviewer's exhaustive 16-bit test found 0 failures.

18. **Why 17 iterations in `__udivmodhi4`.** The text gives the count without explaining it. The entry jumps to `_ep`, so the first of the 17 passes through `_ep` only shifts the dividend's top bit into the carry. The bit shifted into `r25:r24` on that first pass (C = 0) leaves again on the 17th, so the loop body runs 16 times, once per quotient bit. This is also what the cycle count in the answer depends on (17 passes through `_ep`, 16 through the body). Also add a cross-reference to §10.02, which this algorithm anticipates.

19. **Hands-on A at -O2 may not show `__umulhisi3`.** In the reviewer's avr-gcc 7.3, `main` of `avr_ops.c` at -O2 has `div10` inlined and calls `__divmodhi4`, because range analysis knows `v` ≤ 255. `__umulhisi3` is called only from the out-of-line `div10`, which nothing calls. Fedora's avr-gcc 15 may differ, so check before promising: `avr-gcc -mmcu=atmega328p -std=c11 -O2 avr_ops.c -o avr_ops2.elf && avr-objdump -d avr_ops2.elf | grep -E 'call|<main>'`.

20. **`subi`/`sbci` with −1.** One sentence explaining why it works would suit this reader. The borrow out of `subi r18,-1` is set exactly when *no* carry should go into the high byte, so `sbci r19,-1` adds 1 − borrow.

21. **The fingerprint.** "would almost certainly change it" can be made exact for a single difference. Because 31 is odd, 31^k is invertible modulo 2^64, so any *one* changed result always changes `h`. Only several differences could cancel.
