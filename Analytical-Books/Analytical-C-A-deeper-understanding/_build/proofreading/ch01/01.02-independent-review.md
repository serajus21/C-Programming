## C. Technical facts

13. **Hands-on, l. 405: "computed by sixteen transistors in four gates that you wired together yourself."** Category: technical fact. Severity: **error**.
    Problem: 74HC00 gates are not the bare 4-transistor NAND of Figure 01.02-4. The Fairchild/onsemi MM74HC00 datasheet states "All gates have buffered outputs": each gate has extra inverter stages, plus input-protection diodes. So the chip does not compute XOR with 16 transistors, and Table 01.02-3's counts do not describe a 74HC00.
    Evidence: Fairchild MM74HC00 datasheet, General Description: "All gates have buffered outputs." Features: "Wide power supply range: 2–6V", "Typical propagation delay: 8 ns" (https://www.farnell.com/datasheets/88931.pdf).
    Fix: "…computed by four NAND gates that you wired together yourself (inside the chip each gate has more than the four transistors of Figure 01.02-4, because 74HC gates add output buffer stages)."

14. **Gain paragraph, l. 96: "it is why the 'uncertain' band of §01.01 is narrow and the margins either side of it are wide."** Category: reasoning / consistency with §01.01. Severity: **likely error**.
    Problem, three parts:
    - (a) §01.01's band (VIL 1.5 V to VIH 3.0 V) is 1.5 V wide, 30 % of the supply, and Figure 01.02-3 shades it. It is the *widest* region in that figure, so calling it "narrow" contradicts the figure.
    - (b) That band is a datasheet guarantee that covers process, temperature and supply variation. The gain of one inverter is not what sets it. High gain makes the *actual* switching region of one gate narrow (about 2.3–2.7 V in the model), which is why the guaranteed band can lie well inside the rails.
    - (c) §01.01 never calls the band "uncertain". This section calls it "uncertain" here and "not guaranteed" at l. 371. §01.01's wording is "the datasheet promises nothing".

    Fix: "…is what pushes every signal decisively to one side or the other: each gate's actual switching region (here about 2.3–2.7 V) is narrow, so it fits comfortably inside the band that §01.01's datasheet leaves unguaranteed, and the outputs land far outside it." Use one name for the band throughout, for example "the unguaranteed band (§01.01)".

15. **MOSFET description, l. 48: "Between them lies a stretch of p-type silicon with almost no free electrons, so no current can flow from source to drain."** Category: device physics. Severity: **likely error** (imprecise mechanism, and the reader asked for direct correction).
    Problem: p-type silicon is not an insulator. It has plenty of mobile holes. Current is blocked because n–p–n forms two pn junctions back to back, and whichever polarity is applied, one of them is reverse-biased. "Almost no free electrons" explains why there is no *electron* path, but not why holes don't carry current. The same phrase "(very few free electrons)" is printed in Figure 01.02-1.
    Fix: add "…and the holes it does have cannot cross into the n-type islands: source–body and body–drain form two diodes facing opposite ways, so one of them always blocks." The figure label can stay.

16. **Switch model, l. 69: "an nMOS transistor passes a strong LOW but only a weak HIGH … a fact of physics".** Category: first-principles gap. Severity: **improvement**.
    Problem: stated as a bare fact, but the project brief asks for the mechanism.
    Fix: add one sentence. An nMOS conducts only while its gate is at least a threshold above its source. When it passes a HIGH, the output node *is* its source, so as the output rises toward VDD the transistor turns itself off at about VDD − Vt. Pulling to ground has no such limit. The pMOS case is the mirror image.

17. **l. 60: "(more exactly, when the gate is sufficiently below the source, which in logic circuits is connected to the supply)"** and **l. 69: "nMOS transistors always connect the output to ground, and pMOS transistors always connect it to the supply"**. Category: precision. Severity: **improvement**.
    Problem: in a series stack (the lower pMOS of a NOR, the upper nMOS of a NAND) the source sits on an internal node, not on a rail. And pass-transistor and transmission-gate logic use both types differently.
    Fix: "…which in an inverter is connected to the supply"; "in a standard (static) CMOS gate, nMOS transistors connect the output toward ground…".

18. **Shannon history box, l. 150: "showing that circuits of switches obey exactly Boole's algebra: switches in series behave like AND, and switches in parallel behave like OR."** Category: history precision. Severity: **improvement**.
    Problem: this is the modern (transmission) convention. Shannon's thesis used *hindrance*: 0 = closed circuit, 1 = open; series = "+" and parallel = "·". The two conventions are equivalent by duality, but as written the sentence attributes the modern form to the thesis.
    Evidence: Shannon 1937/38: "The symbol 0 (zero) will be used to represent the hindrance of a closed circuit, and the symbol 1 (unity) to represent the hindrance of an open circuit"; "let the symbol + (plus) be defined to mean the series connection" (https://fab.cba.mit.edu/classes/862.16/notes/computation/Shannon-1937.pdf).
    Fix: "…showing that circuits of switches obey exactly Boole's algebra. (Shannon wrote it in terms of a circuit's 'hindrance', so series appeared as + in his notation; in today's equivalent form, switches in series behave like AND and switches in parallel like OR.)"

19. **Same box, l. 150: "It is the same Shannon who, eleven years later, gave us the bit (§01.01)."** Category: consistency with §01.01. Severity: **improvement**.
    Problem: §01.01 l. 213 says the word first appeared in print in Shannon's 1948 paper and that "Shannon credited the word to his colleague John Tukey". "Gave us the bit" blurs that.
    Fix: "…who, eleven years later, founded information theory and put the word 'bit' into print (§01.01)."

20. **Universality proof, step 4, l. 191: "For each row whose output is 1, write an AND … then OR all those terms together. The result is 1 exactly on those rows."** Category: rigor. Severity: **improvement**.
    Problem: the proof is presented as complete, but it does not cover the function with no 1 rows (constant 0): the OR of zero terms is undefined.
    Fix: add "(If no row is 1, the function is the constant 0, which is A · A′.)"

21. **Under the hood, l. 142: "shows up, decades later, as a preference in how processors are designed."** Category: wording / logic. Severity: **improvement**.

    Fix: delete "decades later,", or write "…shows up at the scale of a whole processor as a design preference."
